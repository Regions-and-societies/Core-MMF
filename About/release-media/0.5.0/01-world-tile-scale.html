How big is one world tile?

ADOPTED
6 km across · 23.4 km²
374 local maps

RimWorld never says how much ground a world tile covers, and two of its own numbers disagree by a factor of 370. Picking between them decided everything about how this mod models population.

The game disagrees with itself

Rejected
1 mapif you measure by walking cost

Crossing a tile costs the same movement as walking 340 cells, which is about one local map. Two unrelated parts of the game agree on it, and it is still wrong for people.

340 cells per tile
→ a hex of ~75,000 cells
→ 1.2 medium maps
Adopted
374 mapsif you measure by the clock

A caravan spends 1.32 in-game hours crossing a tile. At a marching pace that is about 6 km of ground, and 6 km of ground is a lot of local maps.

3300 ticks per tile ÷ 2500 per hour
→ 1.32 h at ~4.5 km/h
→ 6 km across, 23.4 km²

Why the small answer had to go

01

A full map holds about a hundred people

Measured from a real late-game colony: twenty-five pawns filled a quarter of a medium map, with private rooms and luxury furniture. A whole map at that standard is roughly a hundred, and RimWorld has no vertical building to go past it.

02

A hundred people is not a region

If a tile caps out near a hundred, there is nothing for a regional model to describe. Raiders, traders and recruits have to come from somewhere, and a world of hamlets cannot supply them without inventing people out of thin air.

03

Demographics need a crowd to be stable

This is the one that settled it. In a population of a hundred, one defector moves the numbers. A caravan dying shifts the region's age profile; a single migrant changes its make-up. Movement between regions becomes noise rather than a flow you can watch. Populations have to be large enough that any one pawn is a rounding error — which is exactly what makes a defection a story instead of a statistical event.

But people cluster

Nobody spreads evenly over 23 square kilometres. A settlement occupies a tight cluster of those maps — called districts — growing outward in rings, with farmland and wilderness around it.

Even the largest settlement uses about a sixth of its tile. That is what leaves room for suburbs and farmland to be real places rather than a fudge factor.

Sixty-one districts is not a stretch

The shaded patch is a city at its largest, drawn inside the tile that holds it — the same hexagon shape, at roughly a sixth of the area. The ladder stops at ring four because that is where a settlement stops being a settlement — not because the tile runs out of room.

Expansion pack · in development

Regions and Societies: Districts

Core draws the world at this scale and simulates the people in it. The Districts expansion takes you inside a single tile — where those districts stop being arithmetic and become places you can look at, read and deal with.

Quarters, not averagesXenotypes and wealth cluster by district, so a city has a rich core and a poor edge instead of one blended number.
A skyline that movesSettlements build districts one at a time as they grow, and abandoned districts fall to ruin when people leave.
Every district has a jobFarming, forestry, mining, quarrying, industry, garrison — and what the settlement produces is the sum of them.
