## 3. The scale model, in six lines

You do not need the reasoning, only the result. It is documented in the Developer's Guide under *World scale*, *District model* and *Demographic influence and reach*.

- A world tile is **6 km across, 23.4 km²**, derived from RimWorld's own clock (3300 ticks per tile ÷ 2500 per hour = 1.32 h of marching).
- A **district is fixed ground**: 0.0625 km², **374 to a tile**. It does *not* follow the player's map size.
- A settlement is a hex cluster of districts. `SettlementTier` is `Homestead(1) / Hamlet(7) / Village(19) / Town(37) / City(61)`, holding 100 / 700 / 1,900 / 3,700 / 6,100 at a comfortable density.
- Those populations are a **target, not a cap**. `PopulationAt(tier, occupancy)` scales them; the ceiling is `BirthrateRules.BirthStagnationRatio` (1.5).
- **Terrain is build time, not capacity**: `BuildDaysFor(mountainShare, marshShare)`, 5 days on open ground to 13 in mountainous marsh. No tech term — vanilla has no power tools.
- Reach goes as **√population**, not linearly.

## 4. What changed underneath you

Four things landed that alter assumptions elsewhere in the milestone.

**#71 fixed demographics being inert.** NPC settlements had zero modelled population by default, so the pressure field held only the player's colony. Sources per region went 1 → 192 and the faction report 1 row → 14 factions. Anything that looked broken in demographics before may simply have been this.

**#61 now has a real baseline.** With a populated field, worldgen went 20.5 s → 42.8 s on seed `biomemix` at 30%. That ~22 s *is* the demographics precompute, and it is the thing to remove. Note the field warms **lazily**, not at worldgen, so profile the panel/overlay path, not just generation. The scatter/BFS/lookup-table plan is on the issue.

**#70 made the culling able to fire.** `VerifyCulling` now reports 192 sources culled to 2–77 per region, zero mismatches. That debug action is the correctness oracle for any change to the pressure field — it aggregates every region both ways and compares field by field.

**The population thresholds in `SettlementSizeRules` are ~30x too small** for the district model (Metropolis-era 240 vs a City's 6,100). Retuning them is **#30's job** and nothing else should touch them. Enabling settlement tiers by default is blocked behind that retune, because tier caps would otherwise clamp settlements far below the modelled scale.

## 5. Decisions that are settled — do not redo

- **Caravan speed is never touched.** Vanilla compresses travel ~19:1 against its own geography. That is a gameplay decision; core describes ground, not travel.
- **Districts are analytic, never objects.** 374 × 119,904 ≈ 45 million. Every answer is closed-form from population.
- **One tier ladder.** `SettlementTier` only; `DistrictTier` was a duplicate and is gone. `Homestead` is both the smallest rung and what an unranked holding reads as.
- **Two triangular numbers, on purpose.** Pyramid cost `(T+1)(T+2)/2`; population-cap basis `T(T+1)/2`. Merging them gives every untiered holding a cap and re-breaks #71.
- **Pressure is exactly zero past the reach.** A rural tail would undo the culling. Open-country population is `HinterlandPopulation`, not a longer reach.
- **The district *layer* is not core's.** Demographics per district, the build/ruin lifecycle, one economic sector per district, structures, unrest, the management tool and sub-maps all live in [Districts-EP](https://github.com/Regions-and-societies/Districts-EP) (6 issues, milestone `0.1.0 District Layer`). Core supplies geometry and the `EconomicSectorDef` vocabulary. New district ideas go there.
